# How the expert readings map onto the Lean proof

Think of a binomial coefficient large enough to be divisible by many primes, yet small enough to bound their product. Alpöge replaces that integer with a family of maps between lattices. The different accounts meet at the inequality that compares their finite-place divisibility with their size at infinity. Alpöge emphasizes how repeated theta lifts recover multiplicity. Rubinstein separates the one-scale inequality from the problem of producing its inputs at the right primes. Zhang explains why the images of many maps must span enough directions and how denominators can consume their gains. The handoff goes further in one specific place: it traces the divisibility and the rank estimate back to the same map. Tao and the joint statement make narrower claims about the mathematical background and the verification. The largest remaining gap in our account is the passage from arithmetic control of projector denominators to a height estimate for that very map. **DERIVED**, from the comparisons below.

## 3. Where each account puts the new idea

Imagine two scales weighing the same object. Repeating a lift improves the divisibility reading, but each lift also adds a cost.
