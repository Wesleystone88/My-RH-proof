# Which parts of the RH source carry the estimates?

**READ** The proof picks a stretch of numbers where a whole collection of arithmetic costs stays small. It spends that allowance in a later inequality. The source shows where the allowance enters, what else the inequality needs, and where the two meet.

**DERIVED** Starting at the last line of the argument and following names reaches about 11 million lines. More than half of the name references along the way have several possible meanings. The number measures how much text a reader might have to search. The individual calls below give much better evidence for particular steps.

**READ / DERIVED** Three classical formulas play different roles. One evaluates an inner product, one compares the sizes of two collections of lattices, and one bounds the dimension of a representation. The tower theorem takes a separate set of inputs. The source does not support a single chain in which those three formulas are applied in turn to prove it.

## 2. Where the arithmetic allowance goes

**READ** The object whose length is measured is defined in `Formalism/GaloisCohomology/Statement.lean:54–78`. The local data fix the places above ℓ and a character lifting the cyclotomic character. The representation `qzTw D e` uses the twist by χ^(−e). `Formalism.GaloisCohomology.Sha` applies `Sha1` to this representation and its local maps. Its `shaLen` is the module length over ℤℓ, valued in the extended natural numbers. Requiring finiteness matters because the later sum converts those lengths to natural numbers.

**READ** The exact quantifiers and prime conditions appear in Appendix B. In namespace `UpperTheory.Top`, `VVle E t B` quantifies over every algebraic closure Ω of E and every family of local data indexed by primes. For every prime ℓ and integer index supplied by a natural number e with 0 < e < t, its Ш-length is finite. For **every finite set S of primes**, the sum over ℓ in S of log(ℓ) times the sum of those lengths for e = 1,…,t−1 is at most B. The same B bounds every finite partial sum. This is stronger than a separate bound for each prime. Source: `Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/ExistsPageN.lean:12–17`.

**READ** `VWin` quantifies over every character ψ. For each 0 < ε < 1/52 and every page function e satisfying x^(2+8ε) ≤ 2^e(x) ≤ 2x^(2+8ε) for x ≥ 1, there exist J ≥ 1 and X₀. For every X ≥ X₀ and every pair q,d satisfying `WinPair` at X^(1/J), one can choose Y ≥ X^(1/J), with Y² ≤ X. For **every real x in [Y,Y²]** and **every** number field E satisfying `IsChiField ψ (e x) q d E`, the preceding bound holds with t = ⌊x^401⌋₊ and B = √exp(x^ε). The pair stays fixed throughout that interval, while the page can change with x. `IsChiField` specifies a cyclotomic embedding and its exact fixing subgroup via a sign modulo 2^(e+2), the character value and two Jacobi symbols. `WinPair` also requires the primes to lie in specified logarithmic ranges and divides d+1 by q and by all sufficiently small odd primes. Source: same file, lines 19–49.

**READ** `UpperTheory.V.Win.window1` is the elementary interval-selection step. Given at most R real marks, B ≥ 1 and U > 0, it selects [y,2y] below U with y ≥ U/(2^(R+2) B^R). Everywhere in that interval, for every mark a already reached, Ba has also been reached. In `UpperTheory.V.Win.window`, the marks are log(e)/Ct, B = Ct/η and U = log X. Exponentiating produces [Y,Y²] on which every exceptional e ≤ x^Ct already satisfies e ≤ x^η. Numerical estimates on the exceptional set and the remaining terms are separate inputs. Sources: `Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/LayeredIsomorphisms/Window1.lean:6` and `Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/LayeredIsomorphisms/Window.lean:7–38`.

**READ** Ferrero–Washington has both a proposition and a theorem proving that proposition in the source. `UpperTheory.V.Published.FerreroWashington` gives, for each prime ℓ and abelian number field K satisfying the root-of-unity condition, an r uniform through the cyclotomic layers K′/K with # (Cl(K′)/Cl(K′)^ℓ) ≤ ℓ^r. The written root condition uses 4 for ℓ = 2. This is the tower rank bound used here. `UpperTheory.V.FW.ferreroWashington` unfolds it, uses `embed`, `cyc` and `desc`, and supplies the numerical exponent. A consumer can accept this result as a hypothesis. At the actual application `UpperTheory.V.HV.Core.of_four_alt` passes the proved constant to `Win.thmBab_of_lemmaK`; it uses that estimate at 401 alongside the count and odd-part inputs. Appendix B gives the declaration, construction, and application with paths. The proof dependencies below the cyclotomic estimate were not independently verified.

**READ** On the producer side `UpperTheory.One.vwin_of_row` supplies `UpperTheory.One.Join.automorphic_window_of_inputs` with the open-image condition, base change and the row. On the consumer side `UpperTheory.LiteratureTheorems.every_character_of_literature_of_vWin` takes the resulting `VWin` as an explicit argument. ## 3. What the archimedean formulas supply

**READ — Rallis.** `GlobalTheory.Published.RallisInnerProductConvergent` has a precise normalization. For ranks A < B ≤ 2A, the stated Witt-index condition, compatible Weil data, measures, a cuspidal subrepresentation and vanishing of lower lifts, it gives convergence and square integrability of both lifts. Its zeta value is the Petersson inner product of the lifts divided by the automorphic quotient volume. In `UpperTheory.LiteratureTheorems.rallis_inner_product_convergent_of_literature`, `rallis_of_dm` takes the doubling method, the convergent Siegel–Weil result and theta convergence. The proof is assigned to the Rallis field in `published_results_first`, passed explicitly at the headline join. A separate lower-level integral identity in `GlobalTheory.IndefiniteRowSideFullHecke.RallisInnerProductFormula` displays the volume factor on the opposite side. The convergent Siegel–Weil identity in this call and the lattice mass ratio below are distinct inputs. Appendix C quotes the two versions and the call sites.

**READ — Siegel mass.** The concrete statement used by name is `Formalism.HermitianLatticesTheory.SiegelMassRatio`. For an unramified CM field in the source's sense, M ≥ 3, and orthonormal lattices of ranks M and M−1, the quotient of their masses is `massRatioRightSide K M`. The right side includes (M−1)!, a power of vInv/π, the square-root denominator and the Euler product `lValEta`. The companion `MassBasic` specifies inverse automorphism-group orders as weights. `GlobalTheory.Mass.siegelMassRatio_holds` calls `siegelMassRatio_of_LI` with binary Hermitian representation and `liStat`. `GlobalTheory.MassAtRecord.massRatio_at_record` specializes the result at the host of a record D. Two calls show the use of the ratio: `GlobalTheory.Fam.MemberFrame.member_frame_of_standing_hypotheses_window_three` invokes the degree-ratio product formula, while `GlobalTheory.Core.LevLow.eventually_le_trace` supplies it to the diagonal hypotheses before proving the lower trace bound b^(m/2) ≤ Tr(D,u). The family-level route in `Formalism.L2.SG.Pf.SzHypFam.secondSizeStatement_of_famP` specializes the ratio and feeds it to `familyBesselBoundFirstPart_of_ranksF`. Appendix C locates these distinct uses.

**READ — Weyl.** `GlobalTheory.Published.WeylDimensionGL` gives finite dimensionality and the product over i < j of (λᵢ−λⱼ+j−i)/(j−i) for both the nabla and delta modules. The estimate visibly used in `GlobalTheory.CovTop.DefSizes.parameters_scale_rank_gap_bounds` comes from `GlobalTheory.T.dualWeyl_dim_formula_duality_bound`. That theorem gives an exact dimension, invariance under the reversed complementary partition, and a polynomial bound for every integer shift w. The application extracts the last conjunct and passes it to `sizes_at`, where log(dim) enters a uniform parameter estimate. The Weyl branching rule in the literature bundle has a different statement. Appendix C identifies the dimension statements and the actual application.

**READ — Tower and degree endpoints.** `GlobalTheory.T.good_tower_volume_theorem` takes six inputs: `Top.Given`, integrability of points, avoidance, `Out.WeilSt`, existence of a positive family, and a comparison pair. Its implementation builds a root tower with a good origin and invokes `volume_theorem_of_inputs`. The volume proposition supplies proportionality, volume equality, intersection estimates and a mean-slope estimate under its stated conditions. The caller `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.Top.goodTower_volume_of_reduced` constructs the six inputs. The tower pair is subsequently repackaged in `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.Link.good_tower_volume_theorem_bis` and `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.Plug.deepStR_of_use`. At the headline join a covolume result appears as an argument alongside the published results and the window. The name `WeilSt` here does not identify Weyl's dimension formula.

**READ — Degree assembly.** `Formalism.L2.DIV.linkDIV_at_words` constructs `LinkDIV_at` from word estimates, frame data, rationality, fullness, and other numerical controls. The degree clause is already in `WordFacts`: line 332 selects `(hW u).2.2`. Following this constructor alone would silently assume the estimate one set out to explain. At the family join, `UpperTheory.Top.shape_closed_link_n_of_green` forms `hBR` from the selected rows and other estimates, obtains `hDIV` via `Formalism.L2.DIV.closed_linkDIVt_of_closed_bridge_kernel`, and passes it to `topDesWin`. The latter supplies `LinkEta` and the window-derived `DIVinN`, as in Task 2. The exact consumer and source lines are in Appendix C.
